Android and iOS apps

The mobile apps let you connect to and operate your computers from a phone or tablet. The phone itself can be viewed from another RemoHop device too: an Android phone can also be controlled if you allow it; an iPhone or iPad can only be viewed, because iOS does not let any app control the device.

App Store (iPhone, iPad) Google Play: coming soon

Control your computers from a phone

Both apps have the same structure: a native shell around a WebView that shows the remohop web gateway. The gateway and the Go core run inside the app process and listen only on the phone's own 127.0.0.1. The session is therefore still an end-to-end encrypted peer-to-peer connection between the phone and the host, with no RemoHop server in between.

  • Remote desktop: view and control the computer's screen, full screen if you like, and choose the quality (native size, or at most 2160p down to 480p), the frame rate and the monitor.
  • Terminal: persistent shell sessions, with helper keys for Esc, Tab, Ctrl and the arrows.
  • Files: browse, upload, download, create folders, rename and delete.
  • Clipboard: move text between the phone and the computer.
  • System information and screenshots.
  • Sessions that stay up: a session lasts until you disconnect it. When the phone changes networks, the app is suspended or the connection drops for a moment, the app reconnects by itself and brings back the open tab; the terminal re-attaches to the same shell. If the system ends the app in the background, the sessions come back when you open it again, with the pairing or password saved on the device.
  • Saved hosts: the computers you paired with or whose password you remembered; show, copy or delete a saved password.
  • Languages: the app and its pages are in English and Simplified Chinese. They follow the system by default; choose the language in Settings.
  • Settings (native): the language, the rendezvous server's address and key, whether relaying is allowed, the device name, this device's identity fingerprint, pairing by password, saved hosts and diagnostic logs; on iOS also a Notifications page.

Let someone see this phone

A phone can be a host too. Once you switch it on, the desktop app, the browser console, the command line or another phone can ask to connect with its ID and password. You allow every request on the phone, with a scam warning; a request nobody answers is refused after 60 seconds. The other side gets the screen (and basic system information) and nothing else: no files, clipboard or terminal.

Current limits

  • An iPhone or iPad can be viewed but not controlled: iOS does not let any app control the device.
  • A phone that shares its screen offers only the picture: no files, clipboard, terminal or sound.
  • No built-in rendezvous server: enter one in the settings (your own, or the official signal.remohop.com), or connect by host:port.
  • No account login yet.
  • Both apps are tested in CI on every change: the iOS app in the iOS Simulator, where an in-app test connects to a remohop host by address and password, runs a command in the terminal and lists a directory in Files; the Android app on an emulator, where it pairs with a real host and uses the terminal, files, downloads, the clipboard and the desktop. Screen capture and control on a real phone are not tested yet. Tell us about anything that goes wrong on your device.

The app and your data

The app talks only to the hosts you connect to, the devices you let connect to the phone, and the rendezvous server you enter in the settings. The identity key, pairing tokens and settings stay in the app's private directory and are excluded from system backups. The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting SDK. The privacy policy has the details.

Build it yourself

Android

Needs Go, JDK 17 or later, the Android SDK and NDK, and ANDROID_HOME. By default this produces a debug APK and runs the JVM unit tests:

mobile/android/build.sh

iOS

On macOS, build the Go core as an xcframework first, then build the project with Xcode:

mobile/ios/build-xcframework.sh
open mobile/ios/RemoHop.xcodeproj

First use

  1. On the computer, open the RemoHop desktop app and allow incoming connections, or run remohop host, and note the ID (or address) and the password.
  2. In the app's settings, enter the same rendezvous server as the computer (the official signal.remohop.com or your own). On the same LAN as the computer you can leave it empty and use an address such as 192.168.1.5:21118 (start the computer with remohop host --no-signal --listen :21118).
  3. On the connect screen, enter the ID or address and the password. Tick the pairing box and you will not need the password next time.

If something goes wrong, see Support. Reports from real devices are especially useful to us.
