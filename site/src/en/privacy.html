Privacy policy

Last updated: {{UPDATED}}

This policy explains how the RemoHop software, the RemoHop apps for Android and iOS, this website, and servers operated by the RemoHop project handle data. The publisher is the RemoHop project. Contact: {{PRIVACY_EMAIL}}.

The short version

  • Your sessions (screen, keystrokes, files, clipboard, terminal content) are end-to-end encrypted between your two devices. RemoHop cannot see them, and neither can a relay server.
  • The software and the apps contain no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting components and send no usage data to RemoHop.
  • Identity keys, pairing tokens, the host passwords you chose to remember and settings are stored only on your own devices.
  • When two devices find each other by ID they need a rendezvous server, and that server necessarily processes some metadata: IP addresses, IDs and connection times. The desktop and mobile apps let you choose the official server operated by the RemoHop project, your own server, or no server (connecting by address); the command-line program has no built-in default server. Whoever operates that server is the one who processes the metadata.
  • Accounts are optional. A server with accounts enabled stores a hash of your email address and your device list.
  • We do not sell data, and we do not use it for advertising or for tracking across apps.

1. The software (remohop)

remohop talks to two kinds of peers only: the devices you connect to (or, on a host, that you let connect), and the rendezvous server you configured yourself (remohop config set signal). It does not report statistics or crashes.

Update check: the desktop app (remohop app, RemoHop.exe, RemoHop.app) reads https://remohop.com/version.json from this website once a day by default, to see whether a newer version exists. The request carries no identifier: no device ID, account or cookie, and the User-Agent is just "RemoHop"; as with any visit to this website, the hosting provider (Cloudflare) processes your IP address (see section 4). The app downloads and installs nothing by itself; when you click "Upgrade now" it downloads the new release from GitHub (which then processes your IP address like any download) and installs it after checking its signature. Switch it off in Settings > Updates; administrators can set the environment variable REMOHOP_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1 to switch it off for everyone. The command line, quick support and the mobile apps do not check for updates.

In its configuration directory on your machine it stores this machine's identity key, the public keys and pairing tokens of saved hosts, the passwords of hosts you chose to remember that do not pair, settings, the clipboard history if you use the clipboard commands, and on a host the audit log. These files stay on your device and are readable by their owner only.

The owner of a host can see everything a client sends to it. That is what remote control means. Before you connect to someone else's machine, make sure you trust them.

2. The Android and iOS apps

By default the apps are controllers. Each runs a gateway inside the app process that listens only on the device's own 127.0.0.1 and sets up the encrypted session directly with the computer you connect to. No RemoHop server is in between.

Letting others reach this device (optional): only when you switch it on in the app. The device then registers with the rendezvous server you chose (which sees its ID and IP address, see section 3), every connection has to be allowed by you on the device, and by default it can only see the screen.

  • Android: after you allow a connection, the system asks you to confirm the screen capture. The other side can tap and type only if you turned on RemoHop's accessibility service and ticked "control" for that connection.
  • iPhone / iPad: screen sharing only, never control, and only while you run a screen broadcast, which you can stop at any time.
  • The picture goes end-to-end encrypted, directly to the device you allowed; it does not pass through RemoHop and is not stored. No files, clipboard or terminal: the phone does not offer them.

Data kept on the device

  • The device's identity key, pairing tokens, the names and addresses of saved hosts, and the settings you enter (rendezvous server address, device name and so on). If you have let others reach this device, also the key fingerprints of devices that asked to connect and when they last asked (at most 256, to mark new devices), and the host's audit log.
  • This data is kept in the app's private directory; on iOS that is an App Group directory shared by the app and its screen-sharing extension, still only on the device. On iOS that directory is excluded from iCloud and local backups. On Android the app has system backup turned off.
  • The web view inside the app keeps no cookies or caches. A host password you type is saved in the app's private directory only when you tick "Remember this computer" and the host does not pair (you can show or delete it among the saved hosts, and it is deleted when the host rejects it). Otherwise it is never written to storage; while the app is running it may be kept in memory, together with a temporary pairing, so a dropped session can reconnect by itself, and it is gone when the app quits.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes all of the above.

Diagnostic logs

  • Off by default. If you turn on Settings → Diagnostic logs, the app keeps a log of its own activity (connection steps, errors, app events) in its private directory, limited to about 1 MB. Turning it off deletes the log.
  • Passwords, tokens and keys are removed from the log. Host IDs, names and IP addresses remain, because they are needed to diagnose connection problems.
  • The log never leaves the device by itself. It is sent only when you choose Share or Email support, to the recipient you pick.

System permissions

  • Local network (iOS): to connect directly to computers on your LAN.
  • Camera and photos (iOS): accessed through the system picker, and only when you choose to upload a photo or video to a remote computer. The file goes to the computer you are connected to, not to RemoHop.
  • Clipboard: read or written only when you use the clipboard function in the app. The content goes to the computer you are connected to.
  • Internet access (Android): to make connections.
  • Notifications: on Android, the ongoing "Connected to …" notification that keeps a session alive in the background and lets you disconnect from it. With "let others reach this device" on, both platforms use notifications for connection requests, and Android shows an ongoing "Being viewed/controlled" notification with Stop.
  • Screen capture (Android) and screen broadcast (iOS): started only after you allow a connection and confirm the system prompt; the picture goes only to the device you allowed.
  • Accessibility service (Android): used only to tap and type for a connection you allowed with "control" ticked. It does not read or store screen content and does nothing outside such a session. You can turn it off in the system settings at any time.

What the apps do not do

The apps collect no analytics, contain no advertising or third-party SDKs, do not read contacts, location, the microphone or the advertising identifier, and do not track you across apps. The apps currently have no account login.

If you install through TestFlight or an app store, Apple or Google handle installation and diagnostic data under their own privacy policies, and may give the developer anonymous crash reports if you agreed to share them. That processing is outside this policy.

3. Rendezvous server and relay

The data below is processed only when you point a device at a rendezvous server. If that server is run by you or by a third party, that operator's notice applies, and RemoHop has no access to the data. When you connect by host:port, no server is involved at all.

The rest of this section applies to the official rendezvous server operated by the RemoHop project, signal.remohop.com (and its websocket endpoint through Cloudflare). The desktop app, quick support and the mobile apps offer it as one choice during setup and use it only if you choose it; you can enter your own server instead, or use none. The command-line program remohop has no built-in default server and connects to a rendezvous server only after you set one with remohop config set signal.

What the server cannot see

Connections between your devices are end-to-end encrypted. The server cannot read or change what you send, cannot see your screen or files, and cannot learn your passwords. This includes relayed connections: a relay forwards ciphertext.

What the server processes

  • For devices that register as hosts: the device's public identity key, its 9-digit ID, its IP address while it is online, and when it was last seen.
  • For connection requests: which IP address asked for which ID.
  • For relayed connections: the IP addresses of both devices, the start time, the duration and the amount of data.
  • For connections through the websocket endpoint: your IP address as reported by Cloudflare, connection times and the amount of data.
  • If you create an account: a hash of your email address, your devices' public keys and names, and the account's signed device list. Your email address is used to send login codes. Login codes are not stored.

Logs and retention

The server logs devices coming online and going offline, relayed connections and websocket connections, with the IP addresses and data volumes listed above. Logs are kept for 14 days and then deleted. Hourly statistics hold counts only, without IP addresses. An ID that has not been used for 90 days is released, and its registration record is deleted with it.

Why

To provide the service; to keep it within its limits (per-address caps and a monthly traffic budget); and to stop abuse. Where the GDPR applies, the legal bases are performing the service you asked for and our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure and free of abuse.

4. This website

This website is static. It uses no cookies, no analytics scripts, no third-party fonts or other third-party resources, and it has no forms. As with any website, the hosting provider (Cloudflare) processes your IP address and request details when it serves a page, for delivery and security.

When you write to {{PRIVACY_EMAIL}}, we receive your email address and the content of your message, and we use them only to answer and to handle your request. 6. Sharing

We do not sell data and do not share it with advertisers or data brokers. The server data above goes only to the providers that supply our infrastructure (the server's hosting provider; Cloudflare, which carries the websocket endpoint and this website; and, with accounts enabled, the mail provider that delivers login codes), and to authorities where the law requires it.

7. Your choices

  • Run your own rendezvous server (remohop signal) and point your devices at it. See Self-hosting.
  • Turn relaying off on your device: remohop config relay off, or switch off “Allow relayed connections” in the app's settings.
  • Delete an account: remohop account delete --yes.
  • Create a new identity and give up the old ID: remohop key gen --yes.
  • To access, correct or delete data about you, or to object to its processing, write to {{PRIVACY_EMAIL}}. Server data is recorded by IP address, ID or public identity key, so please include the relevant identifier so that we can find it. You also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

8. Children
